1Why this plan looks like this

Previous best: ~25–30 marks — qualified, no rank. The three killers, and the fix baked in for each:

  • Incomplete syllabus → hard checkpoint dates per block; overrun cuts depth, never pushes dates; coverage freeze Dec 27.
  • Too few mocks, no analysis → subject mock at every block's end; full mocks from Nov 1; every mock gets a 2-hour autopsy.
  • Speed / silly mistakes → weekdays are practice-only; error log tags every lost mark concept / calculation / misread; January drills the log, not textbooks.

Weak subjects (Prob & Stats, LinAlg & Calculus, ML/DL) are scheduled first, at ST depth where syllabi overlap. Strengths (Python/DSA/DBMS) go last as a speed-pass.

2Time budget — the binding constraint

~12–15 hrs/week: 1–1.5 hr weekdays (unpredictable) + 6–8 hrs weekends.

  • Weekends are the engine. Two theory blocks, each ending with 30–45 min of problems. The weekend alone is the minimum viable week — coverage advances even if every weekday collapses.
  • Weekdays are practice only. Never new theory. PYQs on the current block + 2 GA questions daily.
  • GA (15 marks) never gets a study block — daily 2-question habit + GA sections of every mock.
3Priority rule (DA > ST)

~75% of hours serve DA (incl. the shared core); ~25% are ST-only. If a block overruns, sacrifice in this order:

multivariate → non-parametrics → stochastic processes → AI tail topics → never DA core

ST-only topics demoted to PYQ-only from day one: multivariate analysis, non-parametric statistics.

8Mocks — the rules
  • Subject mock at the end of every block (test-series subject tests or PYQ-assembled).
  • Full DA mocks: baseline Nov 1 → fortnightly through Dec → weekly in Jan (7 total).
  • Full ST mocks: Dec 6, Dec 20, Jan 24 (3 total). Drop ST fulls first if behind.
  • Paid DA test series by end of September (~₹1.5–3k). Add an ST series only if December ST mocks look competitive.
  • Every mock → 2-hour autopsy before the next: every lost mark tagged concept / calculation / misread in one running error log. January revision drills this log.
9Resources

Shared core (Prob/Stats, LinAlg, Calculus)

  • Probability: Harvard Stat 110 (Joe Blitzstein, YouTube) for intuition → GO Classes DA probability playlist for GATE-style problems. Book: Sheldon Ross, A First Course in Probability (selected chapters).
  • Statistics (both papers): Gupta & Kapoor, Fundamentals of Mathematical Statistics — the main stats book. Casella & Berger only as reference.
  • Linear Algebra: 3Blue1Brown Essence of Linear Algebra → Gilbert Strang MIT 18.06 (selected) → GO Classes LA playlist. Practice: CS + DA + ST PYQs.
  • Calculus & Optimization: 3Blue1Brown Essence of Calculus; gradient descent/convexity via ML materials. Thin topic — don't over-invest.

DA-specific

  • ML/DL: StatQuest for first-pass concepts; NPTEL Introduction to Machine Learning (Balaraman Ravindran, IIT-M) for rigor; Introduction to Statistical Learning (free PDF) — regression, classification, resampling, trees, SVM, clustering.
  • AI: Russell & Norvig — search, logic, uncertainty chapters only; NPTEL AI: Search Methods for Problem Solving (Deepak Khemani).
  • Python/DSA/DBMS: GATE CS PYQs (heavy overlap); Knowledge Gate or GO Classes DBMS playlists for gaps; short GATE-DA warehousing playlist.

ST-specific

  • Estimation & testing: Gupta & Kapoor (above).
  • Stochastic processes: Sheldon Ross, Introduction to Probability Models — Markov chain + Poisson process chapters; NPTEL Stochastic Processes lectures.
  • ST PYQs 2019–2026 — the single best resource for ST-only topics.

Question banks: official DA papers (2024–2026), ST papers (2019–2026), overlapping CS PYQ topics; GATE Overflow (free, has a DA section).
