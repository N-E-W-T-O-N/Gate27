# GATE 2027 Prep Design — DA (primary) + ST (secondary)

**Date:** 2026-07-15 · **Exam:** GATE 2027, expected first two weekends of February 2027 (~29 weeks away)
**Candidate profile:** Repeat aspirant, working full-time, General category, age 25. Previous best ~25–30 marks in DA (just above cutoff).

## Goal

- **Primary:** DA score 65+/100 → single-digit rank is the stretch goal; top-200 (IISc/IIT-B/D/M interview range) is the realistic floor if the plan holds.
- **Secondary:** ST paper as a bonus — funded by the shared math core, never at DA's expense.
- **Post-exam options:** M.Tech DS/AI at IISc/top IITs (DA rank); NUS/NTU via GATE score targets the 2028 intake (applications late 2027, IELTS needed — post-exam workstream, not part of this plan).
- **Registration:** DA as primary paper, ST as secondary. DA+ST is an allowed combination per the official GATE two-paper table (verified against GATE 2026 rules; re-verify when GATE 2027 registration opens ~Aug–Sep 2026).

## Diagnosis of previous attempts (what this plan must fix)

1. **Incomplete syllabus** → hard checkpoint dates per subject; overrun rule cuts depth, never pushes dates; coverage freeze Dec 27.
2. **Too few mocks, no analysis** → subject mock at every block's end, full mocks from Nov 1, every mock gets a 2-hour autopsy.
3. **Speed / silly mistakes** → weekday slots are pure problem practice; error log with three tags (concept gap / calculation error / misread); January drills the log, not textbooks.
4. **Weak subjects:** Probability & Statistics, Linear Algebra & Calculus, ML/DL — scheduled first and at ST depth where they overlap.

## Time budget (the binding constraint)

~12–15 hrs/week: 1–1.5 hr weekdays (unpredictable) + 6–8 hrs weekends. Rules:

- **Weekends are the engine.** Two theory blocks, each ending with 30–45 min of problems on the just-learned material. The weekend alone is the *minimum viable week* — coverage advances even if all weekdays collapse.
- **Weekdays are practice only.** Never new theory. PYQs and problems on the current block + 2 GA questions daily.
- **GA (15 marks) never gets a study block** — it's the daily 2-question habit plus GA sections of every mock.

## Priority rule (DA > ST)

~75% of hours serve DA (including the shared core); ~25% are ST-only. If any block overruns:
sacrifice **multivariate → non-parametrics → stochastic processes → AI tail topics → *(never)* DA core**.

## Mocks

- Subject mock at the end of every block (test-series subject tests or PYQ-assembled).
- Full DA mocks: baseline Nov 1 → fortnightly through Dec → weekly in Jan. (~6–7 total)
- Full ST mocks: first in early Dec, then 1 per 2 DA mocks. (~3–4 total). Drop ST fulls first if behind.
- **Enroll in one paid DA test series by end of September** (GO Classes/GATE Overflow, PW, or Made Easy, ~₹1.5–3k). Add an ST series only if December ST mocks look competitive.
- **Every mock → 2-hour autopsy before the next:** every lost mark tagged *concept / calculation / misread*, logged in one running error-log file. The January revision drills this log.

## Resources

### Shared core (Prob/Stats, LinAlg, Calculus)
- **Probability:** Harvard Stat 110 (Joe Blitzstein, YouTube) for intuition → GO Classes DA probability playlist for GATE-style problems. Book: Sheldon Ross, *A First Course in Probability* (selected chapters).
- **Statistics (both papers):** Gupta & Kapoor, *Fundamentals of Mathematical Statistics* — the main stats book. Casella & Berger only as reference.
- **Linear Algebra:** 3Blue1Brown *Essence of Linear Algebra* first → Gilbert Strang MIT 18.06 (selected) → GO Classes LA playlist. Practice: CS + DA + ST PYQs.
- **Calculus & Optimization:** 3Blue1Brown *Essence of Calculus*; gradient descent/convexity via ML materials. Thin topic — don't over-invest.

### DA-specific
- **ML/DL:** StatQuest (Josh Starmer) for first-pass concepts; NPTEL *Introduction to Machine Learning* (Balaraman Ravindran, IIT-M) for rigor; *Introduction to Statistical Learning* (free PDF) — regression, classification, resampling, trees, SVM, clustering chapters.
- **AI:** Russell & Norvig — search, logic, uncertainty chapters only; NPTEL *AI: Search Methods for Problem Solving* (Deepak Khemani).
- **Python/DSA/DBMS:** GATE CS PYQs (heavy overlap); Knowledge Gate or GO Classes DBMS playlists for gaps; any short GATE-DA warehousing playlist.

### ST-specific
- **Estimation & testing:** Gupta & Kapoor (above).
- **Stochastic processes:** Sheldon Ross, *Introduction to Probability Models* — Markov chain + Poisson process chapters; NPTEL Stochastic Processes lectures.
- **ST PYQs 2019–2026** — the single best resource for ST-only topics.

### Question banks
- Official DA papers (2024–2026), ST papers (2019–2026), overlapping CS PYQ topics.
- GATE Overflow — free, has a DA section.

## Risks

- **Time budget is the #1 risk.** 12–15 unpredictable hrs/week makes single-digit rank a long shot; the plan is built so partial success still lands top-200 + a strong score. Every rule above exists to protect the score floor, not the ceiling.
- **ST dilution risk** is capped by the 75/25 split and the sacrifice order.
- **GATE 2027 specifics** (exact dates, organizing institute, any syllabus tweak, two-paper table) publish ~Aug–Sep 2026 — re-verify at registration.
